# Biological Question

## Primary Question

Do malignant tumor cells from ICB-treated patients occupy recurrent resistance-associated states that predict clinical response and nominate druggable vulnerabilities across cancer types?

## Core Hypothesis

Across ICB-treated tumors, non-response and acquired resistance are associated with malignant-cell-intrinsic programs such as EMT/TGF-beta, hypoxia/stress, interferon signaling imbalance, antigen-presentation remodeling, proliferation, stem-like quiescence, apoptosis resistance, and metabolic rewiring. These programs can be measured at patient level and transferred into bulk RNA-seq cohorts using robust rank-based scores.

## Biological Rationale

ICB response depends on both immune context and malignant-cell state. Tumor cells can evade immune control through reduced antigen presentation, interferon pathway adaptation, T-cell exclusion programs, lineage plasticity, stress tolerance, altered apoptosis, and microenvironment-shaping ligand programs. A cell-state map can preserve this biology better than a small gene list optimized only for survival or response prediction.

## Candidate State Families

- IFN-gamma and antigen-presentation active state
- antigen-presentation loss or interferon-tolerant state
- EMT/TGF-beta and extracellular-matrix exclusion state
- hypoxia, unfolded-protein-response, and oxidative-stress state
- proliferative cell-cycle state
- quiescent or stem-like state
- inflammatory apoptosis-resistant state
- metabolic rewiring state

## Alternative Explanations To Test

- apparent resistance states are cancer-type markers
- state scores are tumor purity proxies
- state scores are immune-cell contamination
- state scores are driven by cell-cycle alone
- state-response associations are dominated by one cohort
- treatment timepoint, regimen, or prior therapy explains the signal

## Evidence Strength

This study can support retrospective association, biomarker prioritization, and therapeutic hypothesis generation. It cannot prove causal resistance mechanisms or clinical benefit of a combination therapy without experimental or prospective validation.
